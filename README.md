1. Introduction and Background
QDADS (Qualitative Data Analysis – Data Science) is an emerging research architecture developed by Dr Farhad Daneshgar to support qualitative research design, analysis, interpretation, and governance in the age of artificial intelligence (AI).
QDADS brings together qualitative research methodologies, computational and data science capabilities, human-centred interpretation, and explicit epistemic governance within a consolidated methodological architecture. It is founded on the premise that computational capabilities can substantially enhance qualitative inquiry, provided that their application remains consistent with the inquiry's philosophical commitments, interpretive objectives, and requirements for scholarly accountability.
The intellectual development of QDADS has evolved over approximately four and a half years, informed in part by the author's ongoing engagement with qualitative research methodology through weekly seminars and scholarly discussions on Clubhouse. During the latter two years, these activities increasingly focused on AI-assisted qualitative research methodology, contributing to the refinement of the QDADS conceptual and methodological foundations.
QDADS encompasses a research design architecture, a methodological vocabulary, a set of 23 governance principles, and a framework for human–AI epistemic collaboration. Together, these elements seek to support methodologically rigorous, context-sensitive, transparent, and accountable qualitative research.

2. Purpose and Objectives of QDADS-MKB
The overarching purpose of the QDADS Master Knowledge Base (QDADS-MKB) is to transform QDADS from a privately developed methodological knowledge resource into a publicly accessible, academically credible, continuously evolving, and appropriately governed scholarly knowledge infrastructure.
The QDADS-MKB is intended to preserve and disseminate the conceptual, philosophical, methodological, and governance foundations of QDADS while maintaining clear recognition of the author's original intellectual contributions and leadership in its development.
More specifically, QDADS-MKB aims to:
1.	Consolidate and preserve QDADS knowledge by establishing an authoritative, systematically organised, and version-controlled collection of its foundational concepts, methodological procedures, governance principles, and scholarly developments.
2.	Facilitate public scholarly access by making approved QDADS knowledge available to researchers, doctoral students, research supervisors, educators, and research institutions internationally.
3.	Support methodological learning and application by providing conceptual explanations, research design guidance, practical examples, and resources for applying QDADS across different research contexts.
4.	Enable responsible human–AI knowledge interaction through an AI-assisted research environment grounded in the authoritative QDADS knowledge corpus.
5.	Promote scholarly development and critical engagement by encouraging academic discussion, constructive critique, research applications, and proposals for methodological refinement.
6.	Maintain epistemic and intellectual accountability through explicit governance arrangements addressing authorship, provenance, validation, version control, transparency, and the evaluation of proposed knowledge modifications.

3. Structure and Components of QDADS-MKB
The QDADS-MKB is envisaged as an integrated scholarly knowledge ecosystem comprising three interconnected components.
3.1. QDADS Knowledge Repository
The QDADS Knowledge Repository constitutes the authoritative knowledge foundation of the QDADS-MKB.
It will contain systematically organised and approved scholarly materials, including:
•	Foundational definitions, philosophical commitments, and methodological concepts.
•	QDADS research design architecture, analytical pipeline, and associated procedures.
•	The 23 research governance principles and their methodological implications, and 10 MKB governance principles.
•	Interpretive and computational approaches, including coding, clustering, classification, and AI-assisted analysis.
•	Frameworks for human–AI epistemic collaboration, reflexivity, validation, and accountability.
•	Research applications, methodological illustrations, case studies, and relevant scholarly publications.
Repository content will be subject to documented authorship, version control, and appropriate scholarly review procedures. The repository will distinguish established QDADS formulations from emerging proposals, exploratory concepts, and external scholarly commentary.
3.2. QDADS AI Research Assistant
The QDADS AI Research Assistant will provide an interactive interface through which researchers can access, explore, and apply knowledge contained within the authoritative QDADS repository. 
Its envisaged functions include:
•	Responding to conceptual and methodological questions about QDADS.
•	Supporting researchers in understanding and evaluating QDADS-informed research designs.
•	Providing examples of qualitative–computational analytical integration.
•	Explaining the application of QDADS governance principles to research decisions.
•	Directing users to relevant QDADS knowledge entries, publications, and supporting references.
The AI Research Assistant will function as a methodological support and knowledge-access mechanism rather than an independent authority over QDADS principles or research interpretations.
Its responses should be traceable to authoritative repository sources, with clear acknowledgement of uncertainty, alternative interpretations, and limitations where relevant. Responsibility for research decisions and scholarly claims will remain with human researchers.
3.3. QDADS Public Website and Global Research Community
The QDADS Public Website will serve as the principal public access point to the QDADS-MKB and its associated resources.
It will provide open access to approved repository materials, searchable methodological content, scholarly citations, version histories, relevant publications, and the QDADS AI Research Assistant.
The website will also provide a foundation for developing a global QDADS research community comprising doctoral students, qualitative researchers, research supervisors, educators, and academic institutions.
Subject to the establishment of appropriate governance arrangements, this community may facilitate scholarly discussion, critical evaluation, methodological applications, research collaboration, and proposals for future QDADS development.
This website also provides links for purchasing relevant books and other publications, as well as academic services to the wider community. 

4. Foundational Governance Commitment
The development and operation of QDADS-MKB will be guided by the principle that public accessibility, scholarly collaboration, and technological innovation must remain compatible with intellectual provenance, methodological rigour, and epistemic accountability.
Accordingly, QDADS-MKB will distinguish between authoritative QDADS knowledge, proposed methodological extensions, and independent scholarly interpretations. Modifications to the authoritative corpus will be subject to transparent evaluation and approval procedures.
The repository will seek to preserve the intellectual history of QDADS, acknowledge relevant scholarly contributions, and maintain clear responsibility for the development and revision of its foundational methodological architecture.

5. Developmental Vision
QDADS-MKB is intended to evolve progressively from an authoritative public knowledge repository into an integrated environment for scholarly knowledge access, AI-assisted methodological guidance, and international research collaboration.
Its long-term vision is to contribute to the advancement of qualitative research methodology in the age of AI by making QDADS knowledge accessible, critically examinable, practically applicable, and continuously open to responsible scholarly development.
The QDADS-MKB is therefore conceived not merely as a repository of methodological information, but as a governed and evolving scholarly knowledge infrastructure supporting the responsible integration of human interpretation and computational intelligence in qualitative inquiry.
